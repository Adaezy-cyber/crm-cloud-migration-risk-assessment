# Risk Assessment Matrix

## Overview

Once the risks were identified, I assessed each one based on its **likelihood of occurring** and the **potential impact on the business**. This helped prioritize which risks required immediate attention and which could be monitored over time.

Rather than treating every risk equally, the assessment focused on identifying the risks that posed the greatest threat to the organization's ability to protect customer information, maintain regulatory compliance, and support a successful cloud migration.

---

## Risk Scoring Methodology

The assessment uses a standard **5 × 5 qualitative risk matrix**, where:

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

| Risk Level | Score Range |
|------------|------------:|
| Low | 1–4 |
| Medium | 5–9 |
| High | 10–15 |
| Critical | 16–25 |

Likelihood reflects the probability that a risk will occur, while impact measures the potential business consequences if that event takes place. Together, these values provide a structured approach for prioritizing risk treatment activities.

---

## Risk Analysis

The assessment shows that **three of the five highest-ranked risks fall within the Critical category**, indicating that they require immediate attention before the migration proceeds.

A common pattern also emerged during the assessment. The most significant risks were **not driven by highly sophisticated cyberattacks**, but by weaknesses in governance and security fundamentals. Unauthorized access, cloud storage misconfiguration, and exposure of sensitive data during migration all stem from insufficient identity and access management, poor configuration practices, or inadequate protection of data throughout the migration process.

This finding reinforces an important principle in GRC: many of the most damaging security incidents occur because essential controls are missing, inconsistently applied, or not monitored effectively.

The two **High-rated** risks—regulatory non-compliance and cloud service disruption—received slightly lower scores because they are less likely to occur. However, both remain significant due to their potential business impact.

Failure to comply with the **Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA)** could result in regulatory investigations, financial penalties, and reputational damage. Likewise, prolonged cloud service outages could interrupt business operations, reduce employee productivity, and negatively affect customer experience.

---

## Business Impact

If these risks are not managed appropriately before migration, the organization could experience:

- Exposure of sensitive customer financial information and Personally Identifiable Information (PII).
- Regulatory penalties resulting from non-compliance with the NDPA.
- Operational disruption affecting business continuity.
- Increased incident response and recovery costs.
- Loss of customer confidence and long-term reputational damage.

These findings informed the mitigation priorities presented in the **Risk Mitigation Plan**, where the three Critical risks were selected for immediate treatment based on their potential impact on the organization's business objectives.
